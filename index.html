You can already talk yourself down.
What if you could read yourself down?

Recast turns your first-person emotional writing into a third-person account. Not to hide from what you feel — but to see it clearly enough to stop circling it.

Why this works  →
The idea

Write it as I

Unedited, unfiltered, first person. The raw version is kept — it is the baseline every rewrite is measured against.

Read it as someone else

The same event, one step back. It stops being a wound you keep pressing and becomes a story you can actually read.

Change the story, not the pronouns

That shift has a name — self-distancing — and thirty years of evidence behind it. Grammar alone does nothing.

Why it's different
01

It refuses to just swap pronouns

Third person alone changes nothing. The benefit comes from recasting the story — its meaning, its cause, its ending. So handing your own complaint back in the third person fails the rewrite.

02

Intensity decides the method routing

High emotion gets distance. Low emotion gets meaning. Distance applied to a small feeling just strips the meaning out of it.

03

Four viewpoints, one at a time

The observer. A friend. Your future self. You choose — never all at once, because stacked perspectives blur into dissociation.

04

On demand, never a streak dose cap

Recast runs on real emotional events, not a daily checkbox. The only documented harm case in this literature came from forced daily repetition over two weeks — so Recast is built to avoid exactly that.

05

Local-first and encrypted

A journal is the most private thing a person owns. It stays on your device.

What it is not
×
Not therapy. Not a medical device. Not a diagnosis.
×
Not an escape hatch. Distancing works because you come back to the problem, not because you avoid it.
×
Not endless. Success looks like needing it less.
